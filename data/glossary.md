# Plain-Language Financial Aid Glossary

Each numbered line has an id (G1, G2, ...) that Beacon cites. Definitions are simplified; studentaid.gov is the official reference.

- G1 **FAFSA (Free Application for Federal Student Aid):** The free form a student (and usually a parent) fills out to apply for federal student aid, such as grants, loans, and work-study. Many states and colleges also use it to decide on their own aid.
- G2 **Student Aid Index (SAI):** An eligibility index number calculated from the information on the FAFSA. Schools use it to figure out how much aid a student is eligible for. It is not the amount the family will pay, and it is not the amount of aid.
- G3 **Cost of attendance (COA):** The school's estimate of the full cost of one school year, including tuition and fees, housing and food, books and supplies, transportation, and personal expenses. It is not the same as what the family will actually pay.
- G4 **Aid package / award letter (financial aid offer):** The school's letter listing all the aid it is offering the student for the year. The total usually mixes grants, loans, and work-study, so it is not all free money. Each award can be accepted or declined.
- G5 **Grant:** Money for school that generally does not have to be repaid, which is why it is often called free money. Grants are often based on financial need.
- G6 **Federal Pell Grant:** A grant from the federal government, usually for undergraduate students with high financial need who have not yet earned a bachelor's degree. Like other grants, it generally does not have to be repaid.
- G7 **Direct Subsidized Loan:** A federal student loan for undergraduates with financial need, and it must be repaid. The government pays the interest while the student is in school at least half-time (and during certain other periods), so the balance does not grow during school.
- G8 **Direct Unsubsidized Loan:** A federal student loan that does not require financial need, and it must be repaid. Interest starts adding up (accrues) as soon as the money is paid out, including while the student is in school, and the student is responsible for all of it.
- G9 **Direct Parent PLUS Loan (Parent PLUS):** A federal loan that the parent borrows, not the student, to help pay a dependent undergraduate's costs that other aid does not cover. It is optional and needs its own separate application and a credit check. The parent is responsible for repaying it.
- G10 **Federal Work-Study (FWS):** A program that offers part-time jobs to students with financial need. The student earns the money by working and gets it as paychecks, so it is not taken off the school bill upfront. The amount offered is the most the student can earn, not a guaranteed payment. It does not have to be repaid.
- G11 **Verification:** A check in which the school must confirm that the information on the FAFSA is correct. Being "selected for verification" means the school needs more documents from the family; it is not an approval, and it does not by itself mean anyone did something wrong (some forms are picked at random). The school cannot finish processing aid until the documents are in.
- G12 **Satisfactory Academic Progress (SAP):** The academic standard a student must meet to keep receiving financial aid. Each school sets its own policy within federal rules, covering grades (GPA) and pace, meaning how many of the credits the student attempts are actually completed, plus a limit on how long the student can take to finish. Falling below the standard can mean losing aid.
- G13 **Net price:** The cost of attendance minus grants and scholarships (aid that does not have to be repaid). It estimates what the family still has to cover from savings, income, work-study earnings, or loans. Loans and work-study are not subtracted, because loans must be repaid and work-study must be earned.
- G14 **Institutional grant:** A grant that comes from the college itself, not from the government. It may be based on financial need, grades, or other factors, and it generally does not have to be repaid, but it can come with conditions for keeping it.
- G15 **Dependent student:** A student who reports their parents' information, along with their own, on the FAFSA. Dependency is decided by a set of FAFSA questions, not by where the student lives or whether a parent claims them on taxes.
